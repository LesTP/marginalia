## Future contextual research - non-canonical

These are planning notes, not corpus annotations. They are not listed in
`../corpus.yaml`, are not loaded into the review model, and do not appear on the
published reading page. Promotion requires consulted sources, canonical source
and annotation records, evidence with precise locators, human editorial review,
and normal corpus validation.

### Occasion and proposed addressee

- **Priority:** High.
- **Reader value:** Gives the departure an intelligible human situation and
  explains why the speaker is consoling an absent beloved.
- **Provisional question:** How securely can the poem be connected with John
  Donne's departure from Anne Donne for continental travel, and which journey
  does the surviving evidence support?
- **Likely target:** Whole poem or lines 1-4 and 21-36.
- **Required sources:** Izaak Walton's early life of Donne, a modern scholarly
  biography, and a current critical edition or Variorum commentary.
- **Risks and qualifications:** Walton is an interested early biographer, not a
  transparent record of composition. Do not identify the addressee, journey, or
  date as settled fact without modern corroboration.
- **Likely disposition:** New attributed biographical-context annotation.

### Manuscript circulation, posthumous print, and title history

- **Priority:** High.
- **Reader value:** Explains that the 1633 page is a controlling witness rather
  than a direct authorial fair copy, and puts the displayed witness title in its
  textual history.
- **Provisional question:** How did the poem circulate before print, when did
  its titles arise, and what can the posthumous 1633 edition establish?
- **Likely target:** Whole poem; the title itself is not part of the indexed poem
  text, so a whole-poem target may be necessary.
- **Required sources:** A current critical edition or Variorum, direct manuscript
  descriptions or images, and comparison with a second 1633 copy.
- **Risks and qualifications:** Do not infer authorial wording or title from one
  printed witness. Distinguish manuscript evidence from later editorial choice.
- **Likely disposition:** New textual-history annotation.

### The relevant Donne biography

- **Priority:** Medium-high.
- **Reader value:** Prevents readers from treating the poem simply as the work
  of the later Dean of St Paul's and clarifies which parts of Donne's religious
  formation, marriage, and career bear on the poem.
- **Provisional question:** What poem-relevant context can be stated about
  Donne's recusant Catholic upbringing, marriage to Anne More, religious
  development, and the poem's likely relation to his 1615 ordination?
- **Likely target:** Whole poem or lines 1-8.
- **Required sources:** A modern scholarly biography and specialist work on
  Donne's religious development; Walton may be used as an early witness only.
- **Risks and qualifications:** Avoid a capsule biography, retrospective use of
  Donne's clerical office, or reducing the poem to a single confession.
- **Likely disposition:** Enrich the occasion annotation or add one tightly
  poem-specific context note; omit generic life summary.

### Gender and the fixed and roaming compass feet

- **Priority:** Medium.
- **Reader value:** Opens the tension between the wife as fixed center and the
  husband as traveler while recognizing that the fixed foot leans, moves, and
  determines the traveler's return.
- **Provisional question:** How have scholars understood immobility, agency,
  reciprocity, and marital hierarchy in the compass stanzas?
- **Likely target:** Lines 25-36.
- **Required sources:** Named modern feminist or gender-focused criticism,
  preferably poem-specific, plus relevant marriage and household history.
- **Risks and qualifications:** Do not derive social history from the geometry
  alone or flatten the fixed foot into either passive subordination or complete
  equality. Represent documented disagreement as contested if appropriate.
- **Likely disposition:** Enrich `twin-compasses-return` or create a contested
  interpretive annotation if multiple sourced positions warrant it.

### Valediction genre and earlier compass traditions

- **Priority:** Medium.
- **Reader value:** Shows how Donne transforms inherited leave-taking and
  compass imagery rather than presenting the conceit as an isolated invention.
- **Provisional question:** Which examples of the conge d'amour, amorous compass
  comparison, and Renaissance emblem tradition are historically relevant, and
  what does Donne make the figure do differently?
- **Likely target:** Title and lines 1-4 for genre; lines 25-36 for compass
  precedents.
- **Required sources:** A modern genre study, the primary texts and emblems
  identified by Freccero, and reliable editions of Guarini, Maurice Sceve, and
  other proposed precedents.
- **Risks and qualifications:** Similarity does not establish influence. Verify
  chronology and transmission rather than repeating chains of citation.
- **Likely disposition:** Enrich `quiet-passing` and `twin-compasses-return`
  rather than add overlapping standalone notes unless the evidence supports a
  distinct history-of-the-conceit annotation.

### The retrospective "metaphysical" label

- **Priority:** Low.
- **Reader value:** Could explain why the gold and compass became exemplary of
  Donne's wit and how later readers shaped the category.
- **Provisional question:** When and how did the label become attached to Donne,
  and does that reception history clarify this poem rather than merely classify
  it?
- **Likely target:** Whole poem or lines 21-36.
- **Required sources:** Dryden and Johnson in reliable editions plus modern
  scholarship on the history of the term.
- **Risks and qualifications:** The label is retrospective and can replace close
  reading with a survey-textbook category.
- **Likely disposition:** Omit unless reception history becomes a deliberate
  corpus focus.

### Omar Khayyam compass parallel

- **Priority:** Low.
- **Reader value:** At most, it may offer a comparative image for the compass
  stanzas.
- **Provisional question:** What exact text did Grierson have in mind, and is
  there any defensible historical relation beyond resemblance?
- **Likely target:** Lines 25-36.
- **Required sources:** The exact source text, translation history, and evidence
  for any possible transmission.
- **Risks and qualifications:** Treat resemblance as comparison, never influence,
  unless historical contact can be demonstrated.
- **Likely disposition:** Retain the current cautious evidence summary; do not
  create a standalone annotation without substantially stronger evidence.
